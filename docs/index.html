The book How to learn from it
Complete draft. All forty-seven programs and all six appendices are written, in both languages. There are no stubs left anywhere in the book, so what remains is finishing rather than drafting. What is not done is the part no build can do: nobody has read it, so the 80/80 standard below is printed as outstanding on every build and may not be claimed. Every ledger is computed from the tree rather than typed — printed on each CI run and in Appendix F — so the debt is visible to a reader holding the paper rather than only to somebody who can run the tooling.

Why

You can call softmax and you cannot say why it subtracts its own maximum first. You have trained a model and never derived the gradient it was following. You read a paper, understand every sentence around the equations, and skip the equations.

Every book in this field is a second book. Each assumes a floor — algebraic automaticity — that it does not teach and does not test. This one starts at arithmetic and means it. It has a floor of its own and says so: the vocabulary of the job — model, token, parameter, training run, evaluation — is assumed throughout, and the mathematics is not. And it uses K. A. Stroud's programmed-learning format, which means it cannot be read. It has to be worked.

What a program looks like

A stream of numbered frames, a few dozen to a program. Over half the frames in the book end by asking you for something, and the answer is at the top of the next frame — so you commit before you check. The rate is measured on every build, for the book and for each program separately, because a format that has stopped asking has stopped being this one.

And it sets traps on purpose. Where a topic has a standard misconception the text walks you into it, lets you commit to the wrong answer, then says flatly that it is wrong. Every one is catalogued with its correction and the program that owns it, each phrased in the reader's own voice.

How to work one — the rule at every frame, the loop around every program, and where to begin — is on the other tab.

Every number is computed, not remembered

A numeric value lives in a script, is written to a generated file, and reaches the page as a reference — so a changed number shows up in review as a diff, and CI fails the build when a script no longer produces what the book prints. The one exception is arithmetic the program is teaching: 25 = 32 is the thing you are meant to do, and putting it behind a reference would be theatre. Ten experiments are specified across the book, most of them free and finishing on a laptop in under a minute. Until the one behind a claim has been run, the claim is labelled as judgement rather than measurement — and the ledger records what became of each, naming the pass behind the ones that have run rather than counting them, because a total is exactly the kind of sentence that goes quietly out of date.

Two editions that cannot drift apart

One repository builds both. There is exactly one list of programs, and every user-visible string lives in a language catalogue, so the two editions cannot contain different programs or a label defined in only one of them.

The parity check compares an ordered structural signature, per-frame maths digests, every numeric literal in order and strictly, the two main files' wiring, and — after the build — what the cross-references actually resolved to in each edition.

Where Polish and English usage genuinely differ, the difference is a macro rather than a translator's memory. The rule is the keyboard test: if the reader will type the token, both editions use the code spelling; if they will only read it, the Polish edition uses the Polish form. So tg for the tangent and tanh — not tgh — for the hyperbolic tangent.

The evidence, and the adverse evidence

Stroud's programs were validated above an 80/80 standard — at least 80% of students scoring at least 80% — and the finding worth having is that the spread narrowed, not that the mean rose. This book has not been read by anybody, so it may not borrow that, and every build prints it as outstanding.

And the general evidence for programmed instruction is adverse: a 1982 meta-analysis found the format did not typically raise achievement in secondary education. That is in the front matter, in a warning box, rather than left out. The defence this book actually makes is narrower — retrieval practice and errorful generation, which is what the frame and the trap are for.

One consequence is stated plainly and should not be softened: the method feels worse than reading while you are doing it. Readers rate the method they retain less from as the more effective one.

It has to be worked

How to use this book, the first chapter of the front matter, opens with a sentence to take literally: this book cannot be read. It has to be worked. The format is built so that passive reading produces almost nothing, and four pages in you will feel it.

The one rule

the book Each program is a stream of small numbered frames. Most end by asking you for something — a number, a word, a line of working — and the answer is at the top of the next frame. A row of dots means a word, a number or an expression is missing and you are to supply it. So: cover the next frame before you read the current one to the end. A sheet of paper is enough. Write your answer down — on paper, not in your head — and only then uncover.

That is the one rule that is not negotiable. Break it and the book degenerates into a mediocre collection of worked examples, and every advantage the method has is gone. You will be tempted to break it in exactly the frames where it matters most, which are the ones where you are not sure.

the research The rule is a laboratory result carried onto the page. Roediger and Karpicke (2006) had students learn a short passage either by reading it four times or by reading it once and recalling it three times. Five minutes later the rereaders were ahead, 83% to 71%. A week later they had fallen to 40% and the recallers still held 61% — and the rereaders had predicted they would remember more. A frame is that second schedule with the recall placed at every page turn rather than at the end of a chapter.

Stroud's own instruction to his readers says the same thing in fewer words: you are strongly advised to cover up the following frame until you have made your response, and there is no need to hurry.

One program, in order

the book Every program has the same seven parts, and the order you meet them in is the method. The loop the front matter recommends is read the outcomes → take the Quiz → work the frames → Can you? → Test exercises → Further problems.

  1. Learning outcomes. What you will be able to do, listed before you start with a box beside each, so you can judge the program against its own promise — and come back and tick.
  2. Quiz. Take it before the program. If you score well, work only the frames the questions send you to: the frame numbers beside each question are the route back, and they are the whole point of scoring it. It is a diagnostic, never a score.
  3. Frames. The program itself. The box at the top right of the opener says how many, so the length of the commitment is known before you start; across the book a program runs from twenty-two to sixty-four teaching frames. The Summary and the Test exercises are numbered frames too, and carry the last two numbers.
  4. Summary. Every item is tagged with the frame it came from, like [17]. The Summary is therefore also a route back.
  5. Can you? The outcomes again, one for one, rated 1 (no) to 5 (yes, unaided). Anything below 4 is not a matter for reflection: go back to the frames named beside it and work them again.
  6. Test exercises. Exactly what the program taught, in the order it taught it, with no traps. They score what the program taught, item by item, and they are the instrument the book measures itself against. Most people skip them, and they are the most informative thing in the sequence: what they report is frequently below the feeling of having understood.
  7. Further problems. Consolidation, and more of it than you will want. Work these until the method is automatic rather than recalled — that is the whole of the difference between having understood a technique and being able to use it.

Answers to the Quiz, the Test exercises and the Further problems are in Appendix A, keyed Q, T and P by program. Do not take the Quiz a second time as a measure of progress: answered again it is the same questions put to a reader who now remembers the answers, so the difference between two Quiz scores measures your memory.

Walk into the traps

the book Where a topic has a standard misconception, the text walks you into it, lets you commit to the wrong answer, then says flatly that it is wrong and shows you why. That is uncomfortable and it is the most valuable thing in the format: a misconception that has been elicited and corrected is gone, and one that has merely been avoided is waiting. So when a frame says as fast as you can or before you read on, do exactly that. Guess, on paper. Do not look ahead for the shape of the answer.

the research Two results say why a wrong answer written down is worth more than a right answer read. Kornell, Hays and Bjork (2009) had people guess at questions they could not know, then showed them the answer; on a later test they beat people who had spent the same time studying the answer — the pretesting effect, in six experiments. Butterfield and Metcalfe (2001) found that errors made with high confidence are the ones most likely to be corrected once the right answer is shown — the hypercorrection effect, replicated by several groups since. The frames you are surest of are the ones the trap is built for, and the correction explains the reasoning that produced the wrong answer rather than only naming the right one. Read it for that.

Note how sure you were before you uncover. A miss you were confident about is not a lapse; it is the one the format most rewards.

Pace: space it, mix it, test it again

the research The book says there is no need to hurry and leaves the timetable to you. The research does not, and three findings translate into habits the format cannot enforce on its own:

  • Space the sittings. Cepeda and colleagues (2008) taught over 1,350 people a set of facts, reviewed them after a gap of up to three and a half months, and tested up to a year later. The best gap between first study and review was a fraction of how long you want to remember for — 20 to 40 per cent of a week, 5 to 10 per cent of a year. A program's numbered sections are the natural unit of a sitting; a program finished in one evening is the massed schedule that loses.
  • Come back to the Test exercises. A week or two after a program, work them again on paper. As a score the second attempt is contaminated by memory, exactly as the book says of the Quiz; as practice it is the retrieval the whole method rests on, timed where the spacing research puts it.
  • Mix the Further problems. Rohrer and Taylor (2007) had students practise four kinds of volume problem either blocked by type or shuffled. The shuffled group got fewer right while practising, 60 per cent against 89, and three times as many right a week later, 63 per cent against 20. Three programs on, pull a handful of Further problems from an earlier one into the sitting.

Dunlosky and colleagues (2013) reviewed ten study techniques and rated two of high utility — practice testing and distributed practice — three of moderate utility, interleaved practice among them, and five of low utility: summarising, highlighting, mnemonics, imagery and rereading. The format builds the first two into the way the pages turn. The third is yours to arrange, and the last five are what the book is shaped to stop you doing.

What it will feel like

the book It will feel worse than reading while you are doing it. In the retrieval-practice studies the advantage is absent at an immediate test and appears after two days and a week; readers consistently rate the method they retain less from as the more effective one, because fluency feels like learning and effort feels like failure. If you abandon this book because it feels inefficient, you will be abandoning it for the reason it works.

the research That is measured rather than asserted. Asked how they study, most of 177 undergraduates said they reread their notes, and few said they tested themselves (Karpicke, Butler and Roediger, 2009). Given a science text, students who practised recalling it outscored students who drew concept maps of it on questions of inference a week later, and had expected the reverse (Karpicke and Blunt, 2011). Rohrer and Taylor's shuffled group above was wrong more often on the day and right more often a week later. The feeling is not a signal about the method. It is the method.

What the method is entitled to claim

the book K. A. Stroud's Engineering Mathematics (1970) came out of eight years of programmed-learning work at Lanchester College of Technology, Coventry, where the whole first year of engineering mathematics was taught this way. Each programme was validated before publication to above 80/80: at least 80 per cent of students scoring at least 80 per cent. The finding worth having is that the spread narrowed rather than that the mean rose — the method lifts the bottom of the distribution.

This book has not been tested on a single reader. It borrows the method and is not entitled to borrow the evidence; every build prints that as outstanding, and it will keep printing it until somebody runs the trial. And the general case for programmed instruction is not good: Kulik, Schwalb and Kulik's 1982 meta-analysis of 48 evaluations in secondary schools found results very similar to conventional teaching, with no typical gain in achievement or in how students felt about the subject. The book quotes that against itself rather than leaving it out. What it defends instead is narrower and better supported — retrieval practice and errorful generation, which is what the frame and the trap are for.

What to have beside you

  • Paper, a pen and a sheet to cover with. The whole method is a thing you put your hand over.
  • The A4 PDF for a screen or an office printer; the 17 × 24 cm one if you bind it. The formats paginate differently and nothing navigates by page — every reference names a program and a frame — so either works, and so does the Polish edition, which has the same frames and the same answers.
  • A Python prompt. The console listings in the book are produced by the same scripts that compute every number in it, and each has been extracted from the finished PDF and run. Paste them and change the numbers.
  • The back matter. Appendix A holds every answer, keyed by program and item; Appendix C is the formula reference, replayed from the Summaries; Appendix B records the notation decisions; Appendix F prints what the book still owes you, computed on every build rather than typed.
  • An erratum is the most valuable thing you can send back. A wrong digit in a mathematics book is something a reader will carry. Include the program and the frame number.
