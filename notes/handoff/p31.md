## What was genuinely wrong, and what it cost

### The trap that could not fire, and the claim it rested on

Frame 24's aibox ended *there is a check that costs one extra run and settles
it completely*, having named **shuffled labels** two lines above the frame that
asks the reader to name the check. Both halves are wrong and they are wrong in
different ways.

The pedagogy half is the book's own rule: nothing beside a question may carry
its answer, and a frame's own box is at least as close as a figure.

The factual half is the one worth the lines. **A single shuffled-label run is
one draw from the null, not the null**, and section 4's enumeration already had
the whole of that distribution \dash{} it is exhaustive over every contingency
table with exact multinomial weights, so its *spread* costs nothing and is
exact. Measured:

| $N$ | mean | spread | 95th percentile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.064958 | **0.0869** | 0.2744 |
| 20 | 0.027687 | 0.0397 | 0.1115 |
| 50 | 0.010332 | 0.0146 | 0.0400 |
| 100 | 0.005079 | 0.0072 | 0.0199 |

**The spread exceeds its own mean at every $N$ enumerated**, and about one run
in twenty comes back at four times the mean or more, on data with nothing in
it. That is asserted as the invariant rather than as any one figure.

So the correction became an elicitation rather than a hedge: frame 25 now ends
by asking *how many times do you run it?*, and a new frame 26 answers with the
measurement and a trap box. It is the program's third trap, it cost **one
frame** (38 to 39) and it took the elicitation rate from 50 to **51 per cent**.
Everything from frame 26 on shifted by one; `\teachesat`, `\outcome` and
`\sumitem` moved with it.

The same claim was in five places \dash{} the aibox, frame 25, Summary item 9
(`\result{}`-wrapped, so Appendix C replayed it), section 6's checklist and its
closing line, and T3's answer key. All five.

**The attribution was checked before it was written.** P27 does not use the
word *permutation* anywhere; what it has is the bootstrap and an exact test
whose null is *the two models are equally good*, with every outcome
enumerated. So the frame says the move is P27's **under a different name** and
names what P27 actually does, rather than crediting it with a word it does not
use.

### Two descriptions of what was enumerated, both wrong

- *all 4095 joint distributions on a rational grid.* 4095 is the count of
  non-zero cell **patterns** of a $2\times3$ table with each cell in
  $\{0,1,2,3\}$; many normalise to the same distribution (3969 are distinct),
  so the sentence claimed a completeness the enumeration does not have and a
  reader could not reproduce it from the page.
- *every map from a three-valued $Y$ to a two-valued $Z$.* The DPI family is
  **343 = 7³ stochastic channels** on a $1/6$ grid; there are **eight** such
  maps. The next frame's *it has to merge two of the three values* is an
  argument about those eight, so the page described one family and argued about
  another.

  **Measured, and it rescues the argument rather than weakening it:** the best
  of the 343 *is* one of the eight deterministic maps. So randomising the
  choice buys back nothing that merging cost, which the frame now says, and the
  script asserts both the count and that the best channel is deterministic.

### And the central identity was taken on trust

$I(X;Y) = H(Y) - H(Y\mid X) = H(X) - H(X\mid Y)$ was stated with no derivation,
in a book that marks such steps. It is two lines: expanding the logarithm gives
$H(X) + H(Y) - H(X,Y)$, and the conditional entropy defined four frames earlier

Doing it exposed a gap: **$H(X,Y)$ was never defined.** P29 owns entropy and
does not define joint entropy \dash{} `grep` finds it nowhere \dash{} and P31
used the symbol glossed only as *the uncertainty in the pair*. It is now named
as P29's entropy with the **pair** as the outcome, which needs no new
definition and is what makes the derivation checkable.
