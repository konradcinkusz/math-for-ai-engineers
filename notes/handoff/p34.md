## THE FINDING: 3.1 is a selection effect, and the section that priced it said so nowhere

Section 34.3 opened with *a leaderboard entry at 70 per cent on 1000 items has
a standard error of 1.45 points, and under that program's own correction for
reading forty entries the margin is 3.1 points*, and its closing note called
that *the honest reading of an interval*.

`p27.lb.margin` is `E_MAX * LB_SE` in `code/p27_inference.py` \dash{} the
expected maximum of forty standard normals, times the standard error. It is the
**winner's-curse overshoot**: the top entry of forty equally good models sits
that far above its own truth. Program P27 says so in as many words and prints
the arithmetic, `2.16 x 1.45`. Its actual correction for reading forty entries
is Bonferroni, committed separately as `p27.z.bonf` = 3.227 and
`p27.bonf.cost` = 2.71.

So the section priced the right number under the wrong description, and then
labelled a selection bias as an interval half-width in a program whose own
section 6 is about quantities computed correctly from the wrong thing.

**Fixed by naming it rather than by re-anchoring the section.** The overshoot
is `E_MAX x SE` and the standard error goes as one over the root of the item
count, so every number in frames 17--19 survives untouched; only the label
moves. Switching to the Bonferroni margin was the other option and was rejected
because it would have changed 9798, the halving, `p34.detect.ratio` and Summary
item 5 for no gain in truth. The section title moved with it \dash{} *What a
narrower interval costs* to *What precision costs* \dash{} because the title
carried the same mislabelling.

## And the count it fed did not reproduce from the page

**And the frame says why**, because the reason is worth a paragraph rather than

fix first, at 22 800. The page names the 9610 the one-decimal margin would have
given, and `code/p34_measuring_honestly.py` asserts it, so that sentence cannot
go stale either.

## 12 293 is the independent count, and quoting it for the paired case inverted P27's headline

**The correction is a better section than the error was.** Frame 19 now carries

## Found while verifying, and not fixed

**`figures/transcripts/p34-remix.txt` prints `[70.65, 70.7]` beside a frame
printing 70.65 and 70.70.** That is P05's *same quantity, two spellings*
\dash{} `repr` of a rounded float drops the trailing zero \dash{} and P05's
remedy, format both ends the same way, is **not available here**. The listing's
first row must print 71.5 and 71.0 to match Program P27's committed one-decimal
figures, and its second row needs two decimals for the near-tie; no single
format serves both, because the page itself prints the two rows at different
precisions and is right to. The two spellings are of an exactly equal value
(0.45 x 74 + 0.55 x 68 = 70.7 exactly), so the current arrangement is the best
available and every alternative makes the listing worse. Recorded so the next
pass decides rather than rediscovers.
