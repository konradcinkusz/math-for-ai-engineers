## The one defect, found by running the check rather than by reading

The prescribed transcript test — replay the listing through
`code.InteractiveConsole`, then divide the two numbers **as the page prints
them** — found a real disagreement between §4's listing and the sentence four
lines under it.

The listing rounded to four places and printed `[0.0997, 0.0999]`; the sentence
said the small gradients fall short of `\val{p20.adam.eta}` by
`\val{p20.adam.shortfall}` = 0.33 per cent. A reader who checks gets **0.30**.
Three of this book's recorded classes in one sentence:

1. **It did not reproduce from the page.** F04's `22 778`, F05's `51.7`, P07,
   P12, P23 and P27 have each paid for that rule, and here the operands were
   four lines above the claim rather than a section away.
2. **One figure stood for two steps the listing shows as different.** `0.0997`
   and `0.0999` have shortfalls of 0.33 and 0.14 per cent; the frame said
   *the small ones*, plural, and gave one number.
3. **A bound quoted as a value.** The script emitted `max(_shortfalls)` over
   three scales, which is the P14 review's *bound read as a value* one program
   on.

**The fix makes the next frame's claim visible rather than asserted**, which is
why it is worth more than a corrected digit. `TRANSCRIPT_DIGITS = 6` moves the
listing to `[0.099668, 0.099857]` — the rounding is inside the listing's own
code, on P19's rule — and both figures are now derived from the strings that
rounding produces and asserted against the closed form `eps/(|g| + eps)`. The
large-gradient row still prints `[0.1, 0.1]`, which is the row the section is
about, and the two small shortfalls now differ by a factor of 7/3 on the page —
which is exactly what the *next* frame explains with
`eta/(1 + eps/|g|)`. Observation, then mechanism.

Verified after the change: the listing replays character for character, and
`0.099668` divides to `0.33` and `0.099857` to `0.14`, the two figures the
frame prints.
