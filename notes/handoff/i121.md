# Issue #121 — orphan tails and orphaned cues in the A4 build

**Nothing under `programs/`, `appendices/` or `frontmatter/` was touched. No
frame changed length, no value was emitted or retired, and no pagination
moved. There is no cue walk owed on this branch's account.**

Two files changed: `tools/checkpdf.py` and `.github/workflows/release.yml`.

---

## The finding: the ledger and the issue measure two different books, and the
## one a reader is handed is the issue's

The issue reports **8 orphaned cues and 32 orphan-tail pages** in
`main-en-a4.pdf`, with the parenthetical *"same TeX metrics as CI"*. CLAUDE.md's
ledger at the issue's own base commit (`fdd14cc`) said `main-en-a4` carried
**21** orphan tails, and every merged pass note since reports **zero** orphaned
cues. Those two readings are not a stale number against a fresh one. They are
two machines.

**1. Every artefact a reader can obtain is built with CI metrics.** All three
workflows compile with `xu-cheng/latex-action@v4`, i.e. the full TeX Live image,
which has newtx and inconsolata:

| workflow | what it hands a reader | compiler |
|---|---|---|
| `pages.yml` | the PDF at the Pages URL | `xu-cheng/latex-action@v4` |
| `release.yml` | the tagged-release PDFs | `xu-cheng/latex-action@v4` |
| `build.yml` | the 14-day CI artefact | `xu-cheng/latex-action@v4` |

There is no automated path by which a reader receives a container-metrics
build. **CLAUDE.md says twice — in *Build traps* and in the F03 second review
pass — that "this container ... is the container that builds the published
PDF".** No workflow in the tree bears that out. I cannot edit CLAUDE.md, so
this is flagged rather than fixed; the sync session should settle it. The one
thing I cannot rule out from here is a manual upload of a container build, but
nothing in the repository does one.

**2. The 8 orphaned cues cannot exist in the container build, by
construction.** `make check` and `make check-a4` run `checkpdf.py` *without*
`--cues=warn`, so an orphaned cue is a **hard gate** on the author's own build
and no commit could have merged carrying one. Both CI workflows pass
`--cues=warn`. So the cue count is 0 on the machine that gates it and 8 on the
machine that ships it, and the gate is on the build nobody downloads.

That is the honest reading of the issue's headline numbers: **they are the
reader-facing ones, and the tracked ledger is not.** It is the exact inverse of
what CLAUDE.md leads a reader of that ledger to conclude, and it is the reason
the two figures have never been reconciled.

of pp 61, 86, 91, 150, 227, 257, 285, 394, 417, 452, 518, 569, 700, 855, 866,
899, 908 and 441 names a different leaf now.

This retires the *instance list*, not the *class*. Some of those frames will
still strand and new ones will have appeared; what is gone is the ability to
act on any single item without a fresh measurement on the right machine.

**The last paragraph** — pages whose ink stops a fifth short before a section
or frame. The issue's own judges already refuted it as the documented
`\begin{fr}` reservation working as designed. Nothing to do.

**Both observations about the instrument, verified accurate and not defects.**
Read against `tools/checkpdf.py`:

* P29 p866→867 is not flagged because the orphaned-cue test is
  `if line in cues` — the page's entire below-head content must *equal* the cue
  string. A page carrying other content is deliberately not the same defect,
  and in that instance the reader's answer is on the page they turn to.
* P6 p441 / P13 p569 are not flagged because `pno not in finals` excludes
  chapter-final pages. The docstring gives the reason: a short last page is the
  chapter ending, the next turn lands on a signposted opener, and the last page
  of the index or a generated manifest is short by construction — so a check
  that named it could never come clean.

Both are documented design decisions with their reasoning in the tool. The
issue presents them as context ("what the checker does not say"), not as bugs,
and that framing is correct.

**All ten named instances are pure pagination artifacts.** I read the source
for each: F02 frame 18 (its display and "Is ... that line?" are two lines apart
in one frame, at lines 492–493), F08 line 364, P04 line 310, P30 line 1000, and
the rest.
In every case the source is correct, complete and coherent; it is TeX that
separated the parts.

## Fixed

### 1. `checkpdf.py` told everyone to do the thing the book measured as a no-op

The tool that reports both of this issue's ledgers printed, on every hit:

> `Shorten the frame so its tail fits on one page.`

and said the same in its module docstring. **Shortening is the move that does
not work**, and the book measured it twice:

* **F06** trimmed one frame over two rounds and the cue did not move — the page
  kept ending precisely at the `\dotline`, because every line taken out pulled a
  line of the previous frame up behind it.
* **F10** trimmed three frames in both editions, rebuilt all four, and got the
  same three cues on **the same three pages** — then lengthened the same three
  and all three cleared.

The two-sided rule that replaced it has been confirmed on the order of thirty
times since F06 and has never failed. It lived only in CLAUDE.md's pass notes —
which is the F07 pass's own finding, *a lesson recorded only in a pass note is
recorded in the wrong place*. An orphaned cue is a hard gate locally, so it
stops a build; the person it stops reads the tool's output, not CLAUDE.md's
900th line, and does the thing that costs them a round.

Corrected in both places, with the two measurements cited and the `\dotline`
warning kept. Verified by exercising the print path with a stubbed finding
rather than by reading it: the cue prints fatal (exit 1), the tail prints
reported-not-fatal, and both carry the new advice.

The advice is correctly scoped — the reporting loop covers only the two tail
defects, and the stranded opener and stranded heading have their own, correct
advice ("re-sweep the reservation ... a LARGER number is not a safer one"). I
checked that before changing anything.

### 2. `release.yml` shipped a PDF that nothing had checked

A tagged release compiled with `latex-action` and then ran **no `checklog.py`
and no `checkpdf.py`** — only an inline `grep` for undefined references, which
is a strict subset of `checklog.py`. So a release could ship:

* an error that `-interaction=nonstopmode` wrote a PDF over the top of;
* an overfull vbox, or an hbox over the 15 pt budget;
* a **non-converged build** — `Rerun to get` and `Label(s) may have changed` are
  in `checklog.py`'s `HARD_WARN` precisely because the failure is a *wrong*
  number on an opener rather than a missing one;
* a stranded frame opener or a stranded section heading, which CLAUDE.md says
  are hard gates *everywhere* because their reservations are measured in
  `\baselineskip` and hold under either installation's metrics.

Replaced with the step `pages.yml` already uses, placed before the rename so it
runs on `main-*.pdf`. The cue stays `--cues=warn`, as in the other two
workflows, for the reason the tool's own note gives: its *location* is a
property of the installation, so failing a release on it would ask the author
to chase a machine they cannot build.

**This cannot newly break a release**: `build.yml` already runs exactly these
two checks, on exactly these metrics, on every push, and is green.

## Recorded rather than taken

### The ten instances and the eight cue pages

Editorial, and **unavailable to any session that cannot rebuild**. CLAUDE.md's
instruction is *fix them in document order and rebuild all four after every
edit*; the recorded walks took three rounds (P08), four (Part V), six (Part III)
and seven (P32), and in several the fix moved the defect to another build rather
than clearing it. Ten blind lengthenings across nine programs in two editions
would re-roll every break downstream of each one, unmeasured, and hand the sync
session churn indistinguishable from real edits.

Doubly unavailable here: the instance list is on the wrong machine *and* 36
pages stale — and **this container has no TeX installation at all**, so not even
the ten-line standalone probe CLAUDE.md prescribes for questions about TeX's own
semantics was available.

### The reservation suggestion in the issue's last paragraph

*"a smaller reservation for the last frame of a section, or allowing a section
heading to sit at the foot with two lines of its first frame."*

Mechanism: `\begin{fr}`'s reservation (currently 7 `\baselineskip`) and
`\mfa@sectionroom` / `\mfasectionreserve` (currently 12) in `preamble.tex`.
**Both are non-monotonic**, and `checkpdf.py`'s own docstring says so: reserving
six baselineskips stranded a frame that five did not, because a larger
reservation turns pages earlier and reshuffles every later break. The F04 pass
found the same for the section constant — sixteen strands none and puts an
orphan tail back in two builds while taking one out of a third. So the issue's
suggestion cannot be reasoned to; it needs a sweep against `checkpdf.py` across
all four builds, and on the metrics a reader actually gets. A pass of its own.

### The blank verso carrying a running head — and its standing reason has expired

CLAUDE.md has recorded this six times and declined it six times, and the reason
recorded each time is **"it is nothing to do with [this unit]"**. It is
something to do with #121: the issue names it (*"facing a blank verso with a
running head"*), and it is the extreme case of the issue's own subject — a page
carrying nothing but one line.

It is also worse than the phrase suggests. `\headrulewidth` is 0.4pt, so the
inserted page carries a running head, a page number **and a rule across the
measure**.

Mechanism, so the next person does not derive it again. All four mains are
`\documentclass[...,twoside,openright]{book}` and `\program` is a `\chapter`, so
every program ending on a recto gets `book.cls`'s

```latex
\def\cleardoublepage{\clearpage\if@twoside \ifodd\c@page\else
  \hbox{}\newpage\if@twocolumn\hbox{}\newpage\fi\fi\fi}
```

The `\hbox{}` page goes through the normal output routine and so takes
`\pagestyle{fancy}`. The patch is one token, inserted after the `\hbox{}`:

```latex
\makeatletter
\def\cleardoublepage{\clearpage\if@twoside \ifodd\c@page\else
  \hbox{}\thispagestyle{empty}\newpage
  \if@twocolumn\hbox{}\newpage\fi\fi\fi}
\makeatother
```

`preamble.tex` does not patch `\cleardoublepage` today — I checked; the only
occurrences in the tree are the two title pages.

**Why it should move no page**, and why that is a hypothesis rather than a
measurement: `\thispagestyle` is `\global\@specialpagetrue\gdef\@specialstyle{#1}`
— it adds no material and changes only what the output routine draws in the head
and foot, leaving `\textheight` and the blank page itself untouched. That is a
reading of a mechanism, and this book's own rule is that such a reading stays
judgement until it is run. The P24 pass is the standing warning: its
`\multicolovershoot` note was confident, specific, of the right general class and
wrong. **Confirm it before shipping it** — a standalone file with a chapter
ending on a recto, `pdftotext -bbox` either side, and the page count and every
text-block coordinate identical. Minutes on a machine with TeX; impossible here.

I judged that shipping an unverified preamble patch that reaches all four builds
and every program was the wrong trade for a session with no way to run it. It is
ready to take the moment someone with TeX picks it up.

## Found in a neighbouring file and not fixed

* **`CLAUDE.md`, *Build traps* and the *Program F3, second review pass*** — both
  assert that the container builds the published PDF. Not borne out by any
  workflow in the tree (see the table above). Ledger prose is out of scope for

  the premise under which the orphan-tail and cue ledgers are read as describing
  what a reader meets, and on the evidence they do not.
* **`build.yml`'s "Report the build" step** summarises `checklog.py` into the job
  summary but not `checkpdf.py`, so the reader-facing tail and cue counts are
  printed only into the raw job log, where nobody reads them. That is why the
  32/8 figures had to be discovered by a reviewer building the book by hand.
  Left alone as an enhancement rather than a defect, and outside this issue.
