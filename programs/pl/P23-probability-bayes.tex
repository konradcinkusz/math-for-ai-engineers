\textit{[Opis do przetłumaczenia --- patrz wydanie angielskie.]}

Argument: probability is a measure on a sample space obeying three rules;
conditioning is restricting the space; Bayes' theorem is one line of algebra
from the definition and is derived, never asserted. Payoff: **the base-rate
calculation an engineer must be able to do in a meeting** \dash{} a
classifier with 99\% accuracy on a fault that occurs once in a thousand
requests, and what fraction of its alarms are real. Independence and
conditional independence distinguished, because the naive Bayes assumption
and most \enquote{these two evals are independent signals} claims live on
the difference. Trap: the prosecutor's fallacy, elicited from the reader
before it is named.
